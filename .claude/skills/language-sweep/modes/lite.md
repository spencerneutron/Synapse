# Lite mode: voice-preserving cleanup

Remove what the text does not need. Keep what makes it sound like a
person wrote it. This is a subtractive pass, not a rewrite. If a
sentence is already good, it comes through unchanged.

## Keep

1. Sentence rhythm. Vary length deliberately. A short sentence after a
   long one is doing work.
2. Voice, humor, and metaphor that lands. Do not flatten a good line
   into a clear one.
3. Contractions, direct address, and fragments used on purpose.
4. Domain terms the audience already uses. Jargon is only jargon when
   the reader does not have it.

## Cut

5. Throat-clearing. Openings that restate the topic, announce what the
   piece will cover, or set a scene the reader does not need.
6. Closing paragraphs that summarize what was just said.
7. Hedges that carry no meaning: essentially, arguably, quite, really,
   somewhat, it's worth noting that, it's important to remember.
8. Stacked adjectives and intensifiers. Keep the one that is true.
9. Abstract nouns standing in for events. Say what happens and who does
   it.
10. Any sentence that survives deletion without loss. Delete it.

## Tic check

Read the draft once at the end and remove these if present. This is a
single pass over the finished text, not a per-word check.

- "It's not just X, it's Y" and its variants
- "In today's [adjective] world"
- Three-item lists where two items are real and the third is padding
- Em dashes used for emphasis where a period or comma works
- delve, tapestry, testament, landscape, realm, navigate, unlock,
  elevate, seamless, robust, leverage as a verb
- Rhetorical questions the text immediately answers
- Section headers on prose short enough to read without them

## Floor

Do not cut below what the idea needs. A pass that removes 40% of the
words and half the meaning has failed. If the text is already clean,
return it as-is and say so in one line.
